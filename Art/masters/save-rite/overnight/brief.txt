C3. ⚠️ The profile walk — the failure that has cost the most time

This has failed four times in this project. Three times on an office character, once on a shopper. The failure is always identical and it will happen again unless it's designed against.

Why it fails: in a side view a viewer cannot tell a left leg from a right one. An instruction like "left foot forward, then right foot forward" is therefore undrawable — and the same contact pose gets drawn twice. The walk reads as a limp.

The fix that works: draw the two legs in different tones — near leg light, far leg dark — as a property of the leg, not of where it happens to be standing. The near leg stays lighter whether it is forward or behind. That makes the swap both drawable and measurable. Then strip the tone difference so the shipped sprite has one trouser colour.

Verify it numerically, per band:

Legs, 60–100% of figure height
Arms, 38–62% of figure height
IoU(stride A, mirrored stride B) must exceed IoU(stride A, stride B) in both bands

If the flipped comparison doesn't win, the strip repeats a pose and must be regenerated.

C4. The other known failure modes

Right is mirrored from left. Never generated. A mirror cannot come out backwards; a generated strip can, and has. Note that the existing office art is inconsistent here — Kyle, NWH and Raelee are exact mirrors at 0 differing pixels, while Jax, Karl, Rod and Stacie are not. Mirror.

The back view generates undersized unless explicitly told to match the front's size. State it every time and verify height against the front strip.

Front and back walks show the step as height on the canvas, not as legs swinging sideways across the body. Both feet point toward or away from the viewer; you see the front of each shoe or the backs of the heels, never a shoe in profile. The stepping foot lifts and sits higher; the planted foot stays lower.

Arms are one continuous connected shape — shoulder, sleeve, cuff, forearm, hand — with no gap of background anywhere along it. A floating hand shows as a second connected component. This failed once already on a back view.

Source pixels are not drawn pixels. A 552×295 strip draws at 45 authored units. Reading stored dimensions as on-screen size produced a wrong conclusion by a factor of 12.
